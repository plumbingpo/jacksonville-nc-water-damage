Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Jacksonville

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Sort Save From Remove

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.
